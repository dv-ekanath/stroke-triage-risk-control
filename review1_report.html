Risk-Controlled Threshold Triage for Ischemic Stroke

A distribution-free guarantee on the triage decision itself — not on the volume estimate feeding it.

Note on scope. This restructures the project's original knowledge-graph / cross-attention framing. Verification against the base paper and the prior art showed that approach duplicated conditioning already present elsewhere and cost statistical power the dataset (n=149) cannot spare, while a coverage guarantee on volume — with or without a graph — still doesn't guarantee the decision a clinician actually needs. Motivation

The base paper's real weakness

Three objections stronger than a raw accuracy comparison, found in the base paper's full text.

1

An impossible generalisation claim

Reports leave-one-center-out validation "across 7 medical centers" with GE/Siemens/Philips scanners. The dataset's own descriptor — and this project's own recovery of the real labels — puts the public set at 2 centers, Siemens and Philips only, no GE.

2

A task-definition mismatch, not a performance gap

Their target is core/penumbra referenced to follow-up MRI or DSA; ISLES'24's label is the final infarct on day 2–9 DWI, and they additionally train on two other cohorts. Their reported Dice 0.81 vs. the challenge leaderboard's 0.285 is very likely attribution drift, not a 3× improvement.

3

The uncertainty layer is the actual hole

ECE = 0.130 is called "excellent calibration." Abstention is a hand-set threshold (τ = 0.15) justified post hoc. The clinical safety claim is computed on n = 30 patients against an unmatched historical rate — and their own ablation shows disabling rejection nearly doubles mis-triage.

The deployed system's abstention threshold is an unvalidated hyperparameter evaluated on 30 patients. This project produces one with a distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee on the clinically asymmetric error instead.
Approach

Three claims, each independently reportable

Marginal conformal coverage says nothing about which side of a threshold a patient falls on. A rule can cover the volume distribution well while abstaining on everything, or cover it poorly while every decision is correct — coverage and decision correctness are different objects. Nobody has certified the decision directly, at a clinically motivated asymmetry, with a finite-sample guarantee.

N1 · primary

Risk-controlled threshold triage

A calibrated crossing-probability per patient (conformal predictive system) feeds a Learn-then-Test certified decision rule with two asymmetric clinical risks — missed eligibility vs. futile reperfusion — set by the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline, not by taste.

N2 · secondary

Decision-analytic validation

Net-benefit decision curve analysis extended with a deferral arm, answering "there is no single correct τ" with the tool clinical journals actually read.

N3 · secondary

What n=149 can certify

A minimum-sample-size table per risk level, and an irreducible-error floor from annotation noise — turning the sample-size limit into a reported finding rather than an excuse.
